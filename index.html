  • The Fundamental Principles of Physics

    The article develops a conceptual foundation for mechanics by treating motion as the primary physical quantity. A trajectory is described as a differentiable function of time, while Galileo’s law of inertia identifies free motion with motion along a straight line at constant velocity. Interactions are introduced as the cause of acceleration and as the rule that determines the higher-order coefficients of the trajectory’s Taylor expansion. The article then explains inertial frames through Galilean transformations, showing that a change of frame alters only the initial position and velocity, while acceleration and the form of the dynamical laws remain unchanged. Mass is introduced as a scale factor that converts velocity into momentum, and force is defined as the rate of change of momentum. Kinetic and potential energy are subsequently derived as scalar descriptions of momentum and its change. Finally, the discussion is extended to special relativity, where mass remains an invariant scale factor, but the appropriate measure of motion becomes the relativistic quantity γv. Energy and momentum are unified as components of four-momentum, while classical kinetic energy emerges as the low-velocity limit of relativistic energy.
